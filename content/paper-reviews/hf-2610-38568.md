## TL;DR

> **Automated discovery digest — not an independent paper review.** Based on the Hugging Face AI summary and author abstract.

A-RSI improves recursive self-improvement by refreshing training tasks based on declining learning signals and using independently verifiable program-generated answers, raising benchmark performance without external labels.

## Research Problem

**From author abstract.** Recursive self-improvement requires models not only to acquire useful training experience, but also to decide when their current experience has ceased to be useful. Existing methods often focus on what experience to generate next, while paying less attention to when fresh experience should be introduced.

## Key Contributions

**Author-reported contributions.** Many also construct training targets through self-consistency or majority voting, making supervision depend on the model's own uncertain predictions. We address these two questions separately: training dynamics determine when to acquire new experience, while the answers to new tasks remain independently checkable.

## Method Overview

**Abstract-level description; not independently verified.** A-RSI monitors reward variation and accuracy in training logs to refresh a task pool when its learning signal declines, without consulting validation or test scores. A static library of model-authored programs produces multimodal questions and derives their reference answers; we specify the task-generation contract, automatically verify chosen properties of the reward and refresh rules, and separately audit the generated answers.

## Key Results

**Author-reported claims, not reproduced results.** The training loop uses no external training examples or human-written answer labels. On the primary eleven-benchmark suite, A-RSI raises the performance average from 57.77 to 61.05 with 800 fresh tasks.
